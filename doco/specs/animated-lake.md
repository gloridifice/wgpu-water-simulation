# Procedural lake animation

## Scope

`OpticsConfig::lake.enabled` selects a nonphysical animated lake surface. The Scene panel and `--lake` provide the complete lake preset; the original beach and rocky-shore presets disable the lake. The lake can overlay FFT waves shared with the ocean without advancing SWE, either foam state, or spray, while retaining their configuration values. The independent CPU package and common framework do not depend on this functionality.

## Scene and propagation field

- Sea level is world Y=0, and the lake center is the XZ origin. A positive periodic radially noisy radius defines the outer shore, with exactly one outer-shore intersection per ray from the center. The base radius is 8–100 meters and irregularity is 0–0.35. The scene is a square three base radii wide.
- The lake bed is a bowl-shaped single-layer heightfield with low-amplitude noise; rocks are rough elliptical hill height geometry without overhangs. The maximum of rock and bed heights is shared by rendering, wet/dry classification, and depth; no unrelated collision proxy is used.
- Resolution is 128², 256², or 512², defaulting to 256². Maximum depth is 0.5–12 meters; there are at most 32 rocks with base horizontal size 0.5–8 meters. A fixed scene seed produces the same grid. Overlapping candidates are skipped; the actual generated count is displayed, and every third candidate attempts an underwater rock.
- The depth-mode coordinate is `max(-bed height, 0) / maximum still-water depth in the field`. Fully submerged rocks can change this coordinate without changing the water mask.
- The SDF is the two-dimensional Euclidean signed distance to the wet/dry boundary on the shared grid, positive in water and negative on dry land, with grid-scale boundary error. It includes the outer shore and holes for exposed rocks. The propagation coordinate is positive SDF divided by the maximum positive SDF in the field. It is not Manhattan distance, radial distance from the lake center, or travel time around obstacles.
- Both propagation coordinates are dimensionless; incident waves increase from shore toward the lake interior and reflected waves decrease. Speed is in coordinate units per simulated second, width in coordinate units. Each mode independently saves its inner turning coordinate (compatibility field `start`), speed, width, and nearshore emission coordinate (compatibility field `reflection`). All parameters must be finite and satisfy `0 < nearshore emission coordinate < inner turning coordinate < 1`.
- Low-gradient plateaus and medial axes can attenuate the display; contours may split, merge, or form multiple segments around rocks. A single connected wavefront, real diffraction, energy transport behind occluders, and constant world-space speed are not guaranteed.

## Shared FFT and water material

- Ocean and procedural water share `simulation/spectral::FftWaves`, spectrum generation, and periodic sampling. The same application uses the same `ocean` parameters and GPU output, not a second lake spectrum. The shared module does not depend on SWE, lake, or the desktop framework; nearshore input filtering still affects both water level and matching momentum, without changing display channels.
- `lake.fft_strength` is a dimensionless FFT vertical wave-height multiplier, finite and between 0 and 1. It applies live without clearing shore waves. A missing field defaults to 0, preserving old lake configurations' purely procedural waves; the new lake scene preset uses 0.2. At strength 0, lake FFT is not encoded.
- The overlay maps world coordinates in meters to the shared periodic field. Missing `lake.fft_shore_fade` defaults to true; when enabled, SmoothStep fades over still-water depth 0–0.5 meters and positive shore distance 0–1 meters. Disabling it removes both fades but retains dry-land masking and the final trough constraint. The switch applies live without resetting waves. This mask is independent of depth/SDF propagation mode. FFT horizontal displacement is not used and does not move the shore or rock footprints. FFT and shore waves are added before constraining the final trough and generating height-consistent slopes.
- FFT and shore waves share the lake simulation clock. Pause freezes phase; clearing removes only shore waves and reflection reservations, not FFT. Terrain, mode, or reset operations restart the clock. Applying spectrum, size, seed, or FFT resolution rebuilds resources and resets lake history while preserving pause and camera; changing only offshore draw topology or density does not reset the lake.
- Ocean and procedural water share the optical path, medium, reflection/Fresnel, highlights, and optical diagnostics in `render/water/`; no separate lake material is copied. Water mask, geometry, foam, and supported capabilities remain scene-specific. Shared shading does not imply support for physical caustics or physical refraction in the lake.

## Cartoon shore foam

The lake's independent rendered foam uses a metric SDF to overlay an irregular white contact edge and fading cellular net pattern near the outer shore and exposed rocks, without changing base water color or shared optical parameters. Cellular boundaries use integer-hashed fixed sites, distance to perpendicular bisectors, and smooth junctions to form rounded holes. Multiscale coordinate distortion, slight directional stretching, line-width variation, and a breakup mask disrupt the regular grid; a low-coverage fine net is added near shore, with bandwidth modulated by low-frequency noise. The pattern is generated in world coordinates and backtraced along a radial field pointing to lake center XZ=(0,0); the field smoothly vanishes at the center. `simulation/animated_lake/shaders/foam_flow.wgsl` uses RK2 to integrate backward along a static simplified velocity field at the same grid resolution for the periodic age of each UV set, publishing two metric displacement fields in RGBA32Float. Rendering follows the same “world coordinate minus displacement” material sampling convention as SWE, interpolating wet donors only to avoid shoreline shear from dry zero displacements. It is not a single displacement obtained by multiplying current velocity by age, and does not transport density each frame or accumulate texture interpolation error. Trajectory steps are limited by cell scale and each backtrace length by SDF to avoid crossing rocks or shore. Both UV sets advance with absolute lake simulation time, half a period apart; their individual coverage results crossfade using a SmoothStep-smoothed triangle wave, with zero weight at reset. UVs and distances are not blended. Shore coverage and contact bands remain fixed to terrain; wave height only slightly modulates bandwidth. Pause freezes animation, and the 256-second wave-record time rebase does not change foam phase. Foam replaces local water-surface radiance by coverage, rather than adding emission; diagnostic views do not display foam.

The Lake Terrain panel provides live parameters: `foam_strength` is dimensionless coverage strength from 0–1 (0 disables it), `foam_width` is shore bandwidth from 0.1–10 meters, and `foam_size` is base cell size from 0.1–5 meters. None resets simulation; normal configuration saving applies. Missing fields in old configurations default to strength 0, bandwidth 5 meters, and cell size 2 meters; the new lake preset uses strength 0.85. `foam_flow_speed` is 0–2 meters per simulated second (default 0.35), and `foam_flow_period` is 1–20 simulated seconds per UV set (default 6); missing fields use defaults. `foam_flow_distortion` is dimensionless nearshore velocity-gradient strength from 0–1 (default 1). Speed increases from the boundary into the water using SmoothStep over the greater of cell size and two grid spacings; at 1 it is slow near shore and faster offshore, producing stretch and shear, while 0 retains approximately uniform inward flow and wet/dry protection. At speed 0 UVs are stationary; maximum displacement does not exceed speed times period. Parameters apply live without reset, though editing remaps current displacement and phase. The same SDF is used to estimate its gradient by a central difference over two grid spacings. Foam appears only where the dot product of flow direction and SDF gradient is positive, i.e. distance increases along the flow, fading in smoothly from dot product 0 to 0.35. Thus foam extends inward from the outer shore, is hidden on the upstream side of exposed rocks, and remains on the downstream side. This directional gate is independent of flow speed and does not change underlying lake wave height. The effect has no density state, physical transport, flow around rocks, or historical wake; it does not read ocean foam state or add pale net patterns in open water.

## Waveforms and single reflection

- World direction defines a sector relative to the lake center: 0° points to +X and 90° to +Z. Angular half-width is 5–150°, continuous across ±180°. Ripples are taller and wider at the center and smoothly fade to zero height at their ends; the end-width ratio is at least 0.05 to avoid division by zero. The directional singularity fades smoothly near the center.
- Each emission independently samples direction, angular half-width, propagation width, and nearshore starting point using fixed-seed random channels. `spread_jitter` and `width_jitter` are positive/negative fractions of the base value, range 0–1 and default 0.35 when missing. Final half-width is clamped to 5–150° and width to 0.005–0.15. `position_jitter` is a dimensionless positive/negative starting offset, range 0–0.3 and default 0.04 when missing; the actual start is clamped to 0.005 through inner turning coordinate minus 0.005. Zero disables the corresponding random term without changing other channels' samples.
- Each emission freezes direction, angular range, waveform, speed, and reflection parameters. The incident wave increases its propagation coordinate at constant speed from the actual nearshore start and reflects at the inner turning coordinate. The reflection inherits the original sector, width, and actual starting boundary without adding 180° to direction; only propagation speed becomes negative, returning toward shore.
- Events reaching the inner turning coordinate use the exact simulated time, not the frame time at which the event is discovered. The incident wave holds at the threshold and fades out during handoff; a weaker reflected wave is generated at the same point and fades in, keeping initial height continuous. Reflection amplitude ratio is 0–0.95; it then decays by a simulated-second half-life and does not reflect again.
- Waves are reclaimed after handoff, lifetime, or propagation range ends. Manual clear, natural expiry, scene replacement, and capacity rejection do not create reflections. Final combined troughs are constrained by still-water depth and dry-domain surfaces are culled. This is a display constraint, not volume conservation.

## Timing, capacity, and commands

The CPU f64 clock advances according to pause and speed multiplier; GPU time is relative to a local origin, with time and birth records rebased together every 256 seconds. For the same inputs, different normal-frame dt partitions produce consistent events and wave positions at the same simulation time within numerical tolerance. Automatic birth times are calculated from a beat origin and integer index; stable event order and seed determine random variation.

Total capacity is 128 waves; an incident wave with reflection enabled reserves one descendant slot. A full pool rejects new incident waves and increments the dropped count without overwriting visible waves or requesting unguaranteed extra reflection capacity. Normal operation does not read back the wave list or simulation field from the GPU.

- Pause or zero speed: no automatic advancement or repeated emission. Manual emission can still create birth records, with fade-in and movement waiting until playback resumes.
- Clear: removes active waves and pending reflections; the next automatic emission is scheduled at current time plus the interval, without replaying a backlog. Random sequence and global time are not reset.
- Apply terrain, mode, or seed, or restart timing: resets waves, events, clock, and random sequence while preserving pause. Applying terrain or mode does not reset the camera.
- Edit the emission interval or toggle automatic emission: reschedules the next beat without clearing old waves. Other emission parameters affect only new waves and their descendants.

## Data and rendering boundaries

A GPU wave record consists of five aligned vec4s, totaling 80 bytes. The CPU manages events and reservations; the GPU analytically computes fronts from time. Final height is composed first, then slopes are generated from that height to avoid parallel neighbor read/write races. Rendering and supported optics use the same published wave field; the surface covers only the local grid, without infinite ocean tiling.

The lake supports medium extinction, manual or sky scattering, sky reflection and highlights, SSR, fast screen refraction, and HDR/Bloom. SSR uses lake wave height to select opaque objects above water. Physical caustics and physical refraction retain their settings but are explicitly unavailable: they are not encoded, do not read the old ocean surface, and do not automatically switch to fast mode. Ocean wave-height gain, horizontal displacement, and foam shortcuts do not affect the lake; lake FFT uses its own overlay strength, while shore-wave amplitude remains independent.

## UI, saving, and failures

In the six-workspace Dock, the Lake Terrain panel belongs to Scene and retains terrain and FFT strength. Animated SDF Ripples has its own sidebar icon and includes depth/SDF mode, ripple seed, emission, waveform, reflection, and diagnostics. Old layouts retain the `lake` panel position; a missing new workspace uses the default left/right layout without overwriting other workspaces. In non-lake scenes the new workspace shows only applicability status; navigation does not switch scenes. Terrain and propagation field/ripple seed have separate drafts and explicit apply operations; applying one neither carries along nor discards the other's draft, and switching workspaces does not reset simulation. Emission parameters affect new waves live and diagnostics switch live. Tooltips specify units, ranges, and application semantics. The shared Spectrum and Waveform panel remains available in lake mode, with explicit draft application; lake state hides ocean wave-height gain, displacement, and topology controls, disables offshore mesh density, and instead shows the same FFT overlay strength control. Other inapplicable ocean panels are temporarily hidden with positions retained. SSR and wave reflection are separate.

Terrain/mode construction validates and builds new resources before replacing old ones. Validation failure or an empty water domain retains the old resources and displays an error. Lake settings follow the session and named-configuration manual save, read-only, and no-settings rules; drafts, active waves, time, and counts are not saved. Reloading starts from the initial wave state. Old configurations missing the lake field default to disabled without discarding other parameters.

## Verification and boundaries

`cargo test --features ocean --lib` covers configuration compatibility, deterministic terrain, an independent circular-ring distance reference, submerged rocks, event partitioning, single reflection, capacity, clearing, and egui mode drafts and explicit application. `ocean --verify-lake` executes actual compute/raster checks, wet/dry and trough constraints, height/slope consistency, reflection handoff, angle wrap/time rebasing, both modes and optics isolation/ocean restoration. It checks that the actual wave-height energy center moves inward, and uses an independent analytic traveling cosine to verify FFT world-coordinate/periodic sampling, strength, the shore-fade switch, final slopes, pause, and clear/disable. It also verifies metric foam material-coordinate displacement and direction, nearshore material spacing changes, wet/dry constraints, pause, frame partitioning, dual-phase resets, and zero speed, and separately measures wave-field composition and the foam UV flow path using actual timestamps (the original procedural-wave benchmark excludes FFT). Full `--verify` includes these checks.

Offscreen snapshots fix seed, camera, and simulation time and output only the scene; they do not verify parameter panels. Real window screenshots can confirm layout visibility but cannot alone prove keyboard/mouse actions, persistence, or animation direction; those acceptance states are recorded separately in the active change.
